No camera. No simulator at runtime. Nothing fitted, nothing tuned.

Say this to Mo explicitly

An outer certificate is a pass filter, not a fail filter. His words were “eliminate definitive failures,” which is literally the inner certificate — the one that proves a motion collides.

I'm proposing the pass filter first because the research says it's the higher-value half and the resolve rate will settle it. But that is a substitution, and it needs saying out loud rather than hoping it reads as the same thing.

02

The mechanism, and why it isn't sampling

Sampling a trajectory at N poses tests N instants and says nothing about the time between them. Two arms can cross entirely inside one interval with both endpoints clear. That hole is why the old design needed a travel-bound patch bolted on afterwards.

The motion bound

For a point on link L, if upstream joint i rotates by δᵢ over an interval, that point can move no further than δᵢ times its perpendicular distance from joint i's axis. Sum over every upstream joint and you have a bound on how far any point of L travels during the interval.

bound(L, δ)  =  Σᵢ   δᵢ · ρᵢ(L) ρᵢ(L) — the largest perpendicular distance from joint i's axis to any point of link L

The test, and subdivision

Evaluate forward kinematics once, at the interval's midpoint configuration. Inflate every sphere by half its link's bound. Test all cross-arm sphere pairs. If the minimum gap is positive, the segment is clear over its entire duration. If not, bisect: δ halves, so the inflation halves with it.

Cost is adaptive, which is the good property: an easy motion certifies at depth 0 with a single test, and only near-misses pay for refinement. Past the depth cap, return UNKNOWN. This also yields a second reportable number besides resolve rate — sphere-pair tests per verdict.

03

Module architecture

The organising constraint is a single import rule: proxy, bounds, trajectory and certify import no mujoco. One assertion in the test suite enforces it, which turns “this doesn't use the simulator” from a claim into something CI checks.

What each module exposes

ModuleSurfaceNotes
robot.pyload_station() → Stationchain, axes, anchors, meshes, joint ranges. The only mujoco in the build path.
proxy.pyfit(meshes) → Spherescentres, radii, link index. Over-approximating by construction.
bounds.pymotion_radii(chain, spheres) → ρ(n_links × n_joints). Static, conservative over the whole joint range.
trajectory.pySegment(q0, q1) · midpoint · delta · bisectNo time semantics beyond the interval itself.
certify.pycertify(segA, segB, tables, max_depth)→ (verdict, n_pair_tests). Recursive; the whole runtime path.
oracle.pycontacted(trajA, trajB) → boolAny geom of arm A touching any geom of arm B. Plain boolean.
generate.pypairs(n, rng) → [(TrajA, TrajB)]Must include deliberate contention, or the result is vacuous.

Adjacency comes from the kinematic tree, not from sampling. Zero cross-arm exclusions, ever — a sampled allowed-collision matrix would disable exactly the pairs this exists to catch.

04

Verification, in order, because each gates the next

GateTestIf it fails
1Containment. Every collision-mesh vertex falls inside some outer sphere.Nothing downstream means anything.
2Forward kinematics against MuJoCo's own body transforms.Every position is wrong; the bound is measuring the wrong geometry.
3Motion bound. Sample configurations inside an interval and assert actual point displacement never exceeds the computed bound.CLEAR is unsound and everything looks fine until it doesn't.
4Certificate soundness against the oracle: zero CLEAR verdicts that actually collided.A bug, not a tuning issue. There is no threshold to move.
5Resolve rate and cost.The feature isn't worth having.

Gate 3 is the one that would get skipped and the one that matters. An under-estimating ρ produces a certifier that is fast, confident, and quietly wrong.

06

Non-goals, stated up front

  • ✕No objects, table, camera or scene estimate. Regime A only.
  • ✕No intent, targets or licences. Two arms have no legitimate contact, so nothing needs permission — which is exactly what makes this the cleanest case in the system.
  • ✕No self-collision in v1. Adjacent links touch by design, and that needs an allowed-collision matrix whose sampled NEVER entries carry an unquantified false-negative rate.
  • ✕No inner certificate until the outer resolve rate says whether it's worth it.
  • ✕No force trip. It ships with the arm at 20 N.
07

Two things to carry into the build

The 6 N versus 20 N discrepancy. The old harm threshold sits below Franka's own collision threshold, so some of what was called unsafe the robot itself reports as mere contact. This rebuild's oracle is a plain boolean — any arm-arm geom contact — which sidesteps it. It still needs resolving before any number here is quoted alongside the old ones.

The framing stays. The technique is KCCD and it has a fifteen-year production history at DLR and DFKI. The contribution is the measurement.
